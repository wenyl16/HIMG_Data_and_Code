Fig. 1 | Renewable penetration drives accelerated growth in storage requirements under tighter consumption constraints. Load-normalised storage duration as a function of renewable penetration for industrial (b), commercial (c), rural (d) and island (e) microgrids under renewable-consumption targets of 60%, 70%, 80%, 90% and 100%. Load-normalised storage duration is defined as the optimised storage-energy capacity divided by annual mean load. Filled circles denote the flexibility turning points identified by the maximum-curvature criterion and provide representative markers of the continuous shift from gradual to accelerated storage-duration growth. Increasing the consumption target shifts the turning point towards lower renewable penetration in all four systems. This migration is summarised across all four systems in a: turning points converge within a narrow range of renewable penetration under the 60% target but disperse considerably under the 100% target, indicating that differences among system types become more pronounced as the consumption constraint tightens.
